# Product

## Register

product

> Gaijin Hunter is a hybrid: a marketing/content layer (landing, guides, stories,
> salary insights) sits alongside a working toolset (job board, application tracker,
> resume/CV/shokumu builders, visa & take-home-pay calculators). The **default
> register is product** — most surfaces put the user inside a task, and the bar is
> earned familiarity, not spectacle. When working on a content surface
> (`landing`, `guides_*`, `*_detail`, `stories_*`, `insights_*`, `pillar_guide`),
> treat that specific task as **brand** and read `reference/brand.md`; everything
> else is **product**.

## Users

Foreigners seeking or holding professional work in Japan — software engineers,
finance/marketing/sales professionals, English teachers, and career-changers
relocating from abroad. They arrive anxious and information-starved: visa rules,
JLPT requirements, salary norms, rirekisho/shokumu conventions, and cost of living
are all unfamiliar and high-stakes. Many are non-native English readers. Their
context spans casual research (on a phone, evenings, dreaming about a move) to
active job-hunting (on a laptop, comparing roles, tracking applications, building
a Japanese resume the night before a deadline).

The job to be done: **decide whether a move to Japan is viable, then actually
execute the job search** — find English-friendly roles, understand what they pay
and require, check visa eligibility, and produce Japan-correct application materials.

## Product Purpose

A free, no-signup, privacy-first career platform that aggregates foreigner-friendly
Japan jobs and wraps them in the decision tools and reference content a newcomer
needs. All calculators run client-side; saved jobs and tracker state live in the
browser via localStorage — no account, no data collection, no ads.

Success looks like: a user trusts the numbers (salary context, visa points,
take-home pay), finds roles they wouldn't have found on a single source, and walks
into an application with a correct rirekisho/shokumu and realistic expectations —
without ever creating an account or paying.

## Brand Personality

**Expert, trustworthy, calm.** Authoritative without being stiff; research-backed
and precise without lecturing. The reassuring senior colleague who's done the move
and tells you the truth — including the unglamorous parts. Voice is plain, direct,
and specific (real yen figures, real visa categories, cited surveys), never hype or
salesy. Quietly confident. The emotional goal is **relief and control**: turning a
bewildering, high-stakes process into something legible and navigable.

Visually this is carried by a committed Japanese-editorial identity — warm sumi-ink
on washi-paper neutrals, a single refined hinomaru vermilion accent, an editorial
serif (Fraunces) for display paired with a clean grotesque (Geist) for UI and mono
(Geist Mono) for data. The "Japan" is in the restraint and craft, never in motifs.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS / startup.** No gradient-mesh heroes, hero-metric templates,
  identical icon-card grids, tracked-uppercase eyebrows above every section, or the
  navy-and-purple AI-tool look.
- **Cheap job-board clutter.** No Indeed-style banner-ad density, recruiter-spam
  aesthetics, popups, or dark-pattern signup walls. (We have no signup at all — the
  UI must never imply one.)
- **Touristy "Japan" kitsch.** No cherry-blossom clip art, torii-gate or
  rising-sun motifs, anime tropes — the cheap shorthand for "Japan." Identity comes
  from typography, ink/paper restraint, and craft.
- **Corporate relocation-agency.** No stocky stock photography, stiff blue-chip
  consultancy tone, or soulless enterprise polish.

## Design Principles

1. **The tool disappears into the task.** This is product-first. Familiar,
   trustworthy affordances (filters, tables, tracker, builders) beat novelty.
   Delight is saved for moments, not sprayed across pages.
2. **Earn trust with specificity.** Real yen figures, cited surveys, named visa
   categories, transparent provenance. Never vague reassurance; show the numbers
   and where they came from. Trust is the product.
3. **Your data is yours.** No signup, no tracking, client-side calculators,
   localStorage state. The privacy promise is a feature — surface it, never
   contradict it with account-shaped UI.
4. **Quiet Japan, never kitsch.** The cultural identity lives in restraint, ink/
   paper warmth, and editorial typography — not in motifs. If it could be a
   souvenir-shop graphic, it's wrong.
5. **Legible to the anxious and the non-native.** Plain language, clear hierarchy,
   strong contrast, and reduced cognitive load. The user is often stressed and
   reading in a second language; the interface must lower the temperature.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target **WCAG 2.2 AA**: ≥4.5:1 body contrast (≥3:1 large text), visible
keyboard focus, full keyboard paths, and `prefers-reduced-motion` alternatives on
every animation. Extra care for the audience: semantic state colors that survive
color-blindness (don't rely on hue alone), and plain, jargon-defined language for
non-native English readers.
